# Profile and Positioning

> Who the site says Deepayan is, in one line and in three paragraphs, plus the contact
> channels it may publish.

## The positioning decision

The site presents a **product and analytics leader who builds**. That is a deliberate choice
against the alternative the research documents assumed — see
[research-synthesis](research-synthesis.md) — and it is the one the evidence supports.

The reasoning: the resume is six years of AI/ML product delivery, then an MBA in business
analytics and supply chain, then analytics and consulting work. That is the spine. What makes
it more than a standard MBA profile is that he still ships software himself — two production
sites, one of them a client's, both live and both his own build. Neither half alone is the
story. A pure-engineer framing throws away the MBA, the consulting and the Lean Six Sigma
credential; a pure-MBA framing throws away the thing that makes him unusual in that pool.

**Headline:** Product and analytics leader who builds.

**Value line (draft, to be refined against [my-writing-style](../../CLAUDE.md) when the hero is
written):** Six years building AI/ML products, an MBA in analytics and supply chain, and a habit
of shipping the tools myself.

Four facts, all verifiable, chosen because each one answers a different objection:

| Fact | What it answers |
|---|---|
| MBA, William & Mary, 2026 — Beta Gamma Sigma | Credentialed, top of cohort |
| Six years AI/ML product engineering | Not a career-changer with no depth |
| A 226-respondent survey designed and analysed end to end | Does the analytical work himself |
| Two production sites shipped and live | Still builds |

## Voice

First person, plain, specific. No superlatives he cannot evidence. Where a number is his own
recollection rather than a retained record, the page says so — see
[content-guardrails](content-guardrails.md). The credibility of the whole site rests on the
reader believing the numbers, which means the marked-as-unverified ones are an asset, not an
embarrassment.
