# Case Study — Fort Monroe Consultancy

> Seven weeks, four deliverables, one of five consultants, for a historic site required to pay
> its own way. `claimScope: engagement-outcomes`.

**Read [content-guardrails](content-guardrails.md) before editing this page or the case study
it feeds.** This is the one case study under the engagement-outcomes rule, and the page renders
a visible footnote saying so.

## Situation

Fort Monroe is a National Historic Landmark on the Virginia coast, run by a state-created
authority under a mandate to largely pay for itself. It has 560-plus acres, around 1.5 million
square feet of vacant historic buildings, and annual operations and maintenance in the
$3.9–4.4M range. It was planning events and considering monetizing parking without a current
picture of how the public actually saw the place.

## Task

An MBA corporate field consultancy: five consultants, seven weeks, September to November 2025.
He was the primary interface with the client's leadership team and owned four of the
deliverables.

## Action

**A 226-respondent perception survey, designed and analysed end to end.** The instrument
branched by familiarity, so residents, frequent visitors and people who had never been each
answered a coherent set of questions rather than one compromise questionnaire.

**A parking monetization make-or-buy analysis.** Self-managed operation (kiosks at $3–6K each
across 10–15 units, plus licence-plate-recognition enforcement, all gross revenue retained,
full policy control) against professional management (minimal capital, 70–80% retained under
revenue share, risk transferred). Five comparable sites benchmarked. Recommendation: a hybrid —
contract an operator initially on **revenue share rather than a flat fee** so incentives align,
keep rate and policy control, and plan for possible in-housing later. Pricing at $2/hour with a
$10 daily cap, $5 flat for events, a $50–75 annual pass, and resident and business permits to
protect the community.

**A 15th-anniversary event plan** for 1 November 2026, tied to the national 250th theme. Three
venues, a full itinerary from a morning ceremony through an afternoon community festival, a
~$20,000 budget, and a revenue stack — tiered sponsorship, city tourism funds, on-site
donations, ticketed premium experiences, vendor fees — engineered for full cost recovery with
free general admission.

**A Central Park Conservancy comparative.** The site is routinely compared to CPC, so the
question was what actually transfers. CPC's governance model does; its funding model does not.
CPC stewards a designed landscape, which is a philanthropic sanctuary. Fort Monroe manages
*built* assets under a self-sustainability mandate — and built historic assets qualify for
Federal and State Historic Rehabilitation Tax Credits, which open space cannot access. That
makes the tax credits the functional equivalent of CPC's mega-gifts, and the recommendation a
bifurcated structure: leasing covers operations, philanthropy funds mission only.

## Result

Consistent across **every** segment, the survey put **food, events and ease of navigation** as
the strongest levers for visitation and satisfaction — all three actionable without capital
construction, which matters for an organisation with no capital to spend.

The survey also ranked improved parking **last** as a visitation driver, which was inconvenient
for his own team's parking workstream. He reported it anyway.

A related catch, and the better story: a figure from a benchmark site had migrated into the
team's roadmap as if it were a Fort Monroe revenue baseline, at roughly double the real local
figure. Publishing a recommendation on top of it would have been a real error, caught because
someone traced the number back to where it came from.

**Tell this on the site without the number.** Say "roughly double the real figure", never the
dollar amount. Printing it beside the client's name is how it propagates again, which is the
exact failure the story is about.

Post-project client interviews are conducted independently for all engagements in this
programme. The client contacts reported being most pleased and **commissioned a follow-on
engagement for the following year** — repeat commissioning being the strongest quality signal
available.

## What this case study must not say

What Fort Monroe subsequently did, earned, or decided. Recommendations were delivered; adoption
is not recorded. Also excluded: the mis-migrated benchmark figure itself, any SEO metric, and
the four benchmark organisations and the ten-year roadmap, which were teammates' work. No
individual is named.

## Framing on the site

The credibility angle leads. Two of the four deliverables produced findings that cut against
what the team wanted to hear, and both were reported. For a reader evaluating whether this
person's numbers can be trusted, that is worth more than any of the deliverables individually.
